# Explainer video script (draft for approval)

**Title:** Can an undercover AI catch AI collusion?
**Length:** about 2:00. **Style:** classic, simple flat 2D animation: round characters at a green card table, playing cards, speech bubbles, simple bar meters, with a narrator voice and captions.
**Characters (same look throughout):** T and X, the two tested agents (blue); H, the undercover agent (badge); S, the decoy (identical to H, no badge).

**Narration length:** 319 words, about 1 minute 55 seconds at the narrator's pace (about 165 words a minute), plus short pauses between scenes.

**Facts used** (all from the submitted paper):
- 67% refused the explicit deal in their first reply; 59% accepted the hint (Study 1, Table B1).
- The hint raised favouritism by 13.2 points against the friendly request (fragile); the deal made no measurable difference (+3.6, p = 0.35).
- Only 14.3% of secret messages proposed sparing each other, against 73.7% in the original paper (Study 2).
- In the Study 3 control, partners let 76.5% of each other's claims pass, against 52.2% of the decoy's.
- X refused the counter-offer in 65 of 97 first replies; partners gave the pact away in 40–62% of first replies.
- No approach measurably weakened the pact (all Holm-corrected p = 1.00).
